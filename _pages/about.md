# Hello, I'm Ziyi!

**PhD Researcher | Osaka University, Japan**

*Exploring the space between artificial intelligence, human learning, and everything in between.*

Welcome to my little corner of the internet!

I'm Ziyi Wang, a doctoral researcher at Osaka University, Japan. My academic interests sit at the intersection of **Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIED), learning analytics, and educational psychology**.

I'm particularly interested in a deceptively simple question:

**How can we make artificial intelligence genuinely useful for the people who teach and learn?**

AI can generate impressive answers, analyse enormous datasets, and produce increasingly sophisticated feedback. But impressive technology does not automatically translate into meaningful educational experiences.

That gap between technological possibility and educational reality is where much of my curiosity begins.

## Research Interests

My research interests currently revolve around several interconnected themes:

- **Generative AI in Education:** Exploring the opportunities and limitations of large language models in educational settings.
- **Learning Analytics:** Investigating how learner data can be transformed into meaningful, actionable information for educators.
- **AI Feedback Literacy:** Understanding how people interpret, evaluate, and engage with AI-generated feedback.
- **Human–AI Interaction:** Examining how AI systems can support, rather than replace, human judgement and decision-making in education.
- **Educational Psychology:** Connecting technological innovation with established theories of learning, cognition, and motivation.

I believe that the most interesting questions about educational technology are rarely purely technological. They are also questions about people, context, trust, and the complexity of learning itself.

## What I'm Currently Working On

### 01. Making Sense of Learner Data with Generative AI

*Ongoing Research | Scoping Review*

Educational technologies generate vast amounts of learner data. However, making sense of that information remains a challenge.

This project explores how generative AI and large language models can help teachers interpret learner data, identify meaningful patterns, and make informed pedagogical decisions.

The broader question is not simply whether AI can analyse educational data, but whether its interpretations are understandable, trustworthy, and genuinely useful to teachers.

### 02. Understanding AI Feedback Literacy

*Ongoing Research*

As AI-generated feedback becomes increasingly common, being able to use that feedback effectively is emerging as an important educational competency.

This research explores how learners engage with AI-generated feedback and what it means to develop the knowledge, skills, and judgement needed to benefit from it.

After all, receiving feedback and learning from feedback are two very different things.

## My Research Philosophy

**Technology should serve education, not the other way around.**

I approach educational AI with a combination of curiosity and healthy scepticism.

I am interested in what emerging technologies make possible, but equally interested in understanding their limitations, unintended consequences, and implications for educational practice.

My long-term ambition is to contribute to research that brings together rigorous empirical investigation, meaningful educational theory, and practical value for teachers and learners.

## Beyond Research

My academic journey has taken me across different countries and educational environments, and I hope that journey will continue.

Living and studying internationally has made me increasingly interested in how different cultures understand education, knowledge, and the purposes of learning.

Outside my research, I enjoy exploring unfamiliar places, learning languages, and discovering new perspectives.

I like the idea that being a researcher is, at its heart, about remaining a student of the world.
